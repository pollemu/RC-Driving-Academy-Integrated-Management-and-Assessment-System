Driver's Manual Companion

A study companion based on the LTO Filipino Driver's Manual — organized by course.

I
Chapter One · Theoretical Driving Course

Reading the Road

Before you touch a wheel, you need to be able to read a road. This chapter covers the signs, markings, and right-of-way rules every Philippine driver is tested on.

A. Road Traffic Signs

Philippine road signs fall into seven official families: Regulatory signs that command or prohibit, Warning signs that flag hazards ahead, Guide / Informative signs for directions and distances, Expressway signs for controlled-access roads, Traffic Instruction signs, Hazard Markers, and Roadwork signs. Learn each family by its shape and color first — octagons mean stop, diamonds mean caution, rectangles mean information — it's faster than memorizing every sign one by one.

B. Pavement Markings

Lines on the road carry instructions too. Longitudinal lines — broken, solid, or double — tell you whether it's safe to overtake or change lanes. Traverse lines, like stop lines and pedestrian crossings, tell you where to wait. Object markings flag obstructions such as islands, median strips, or bridge piers.

C. Right of Way

  • First to stop, first to go. At an uncontrolled intersection, whoever arrives first has priority.
  • Pedestrians on a marked lane always have the right of way — even where there's no signal.
  • Emergency vehicles — ambulances and fire trucks running lights and sirens — are given way immediately.

D. Key Traffic Laws to Know

RA 4136Land Transportation & Traffic Code
RA 10054Motorcycle Helmet Act
RA 10586Anti-Drunk & Drugged Driving Act
RA 10913Anti-Distracted Driving Act
Good to know. A Student Permit (SP) is valid for one year from the date it's issued. Complete your practical course within that year, or you'll need to reapply.

II
Chapter Two · Practical Driving Course

Behind the Wheel

Theory gets you ready. This chapter is what actually happens once you're in the driver's seat — from your first pre-drive check to handling an emergency.

A. Before You Start

  • Walk-around check. Look over the body, underchassis, and tires — spot anything leaking, loose, or worn before you turn the key.
  • Cabin setup. Doors closed and locked, seat and mirrors adjusted to you, seatbelt on — every time, no exceptions.
  • Know your controls. Window switches, turn signal lever, wipers, aircon, and the hazard light switch, so your hand finds them without looking.

B. On the Road

Starting off, driving ahead, and stopping smoothly builds the foundation for everything else. Choose your speed for the situation, not just the limit — traffic, weather, and visibility all matter. Uphill and downhill driving, lane shifts, and U-turns each have a technique, and overtaking safely means checking mirrors, signaling early, and never assuming the other driver has seen you.

C. Parking

Practice parking on level ground, uphill, and downhill, both with and without a curb — each situation uses the handbrake a little differently.

D. When Something Goes Wrong

  • Tire blowout. Ease off the gas, avoid braking hard, steer straight, and let the car slow on its own.
  • Brake fade or loss. Pump the brakes, downshift, and apply the handbrake gradually if needed.
  • Overheating. Pull over, turn off the engine, and let it cool before checking any fluids.
Good to know. Both TDC and PDC are required before your practical driving test — no matter how much experience you already have behind the wheel.

III
Chapter Three · Practical Driving Course

Two Wheels, Full Focus

Riding demands more balance and more awareness than driving a car — there's no metal frame between you and the road.

A. Gear Up, Every Time

A helmet meeting Philippine safety standards is required by law for rider and passenger alike under the Motorcycle Helmet Act (RA 10054). Gloves, a proper riding jacket, and closed shoes aren't optional extras — they're your only protection in a low-speed fall.

B. Balance and Control

Low-speed maneuvers and figure-8 drills build the core skill of riding: staying balanced below walking pace. Get a feel for the throttle-clutch relationship in a controlled area before you ever ride in traffic.

C. Braking

Use both brakes together — mostly front, a little rear — for the shortest, most controlled stop. Grabbing the front brake alone at speed can throw you over the handlebars.

D. Riding Visible

  • Position yourself where car mirrors can actually see you, not in a driver's blind spot.
  • Ride as if you're invisible until a driver clearly acknowledges you.

E. Know the Law

The Children's Safety on Motorcycles Act (RA 10666) prohibits carrying a child too small to reach the footpegs or wrap their arms around the rider's waist.

Good to know. A motorcycle license (Code A/A1) doesn't cover cars, and a car license doesn't automatically cover motorcycles — check which code your training certificate applies to.

IV
Chapter Four · Road Courtesy & Safety

Staying Safe, Staying Legal

Good driving isn't just skill — it's judgment, patience, and knowing what's at stake if you get it wrong.

A. Drive Defensively

Assume every other driver might make a mistake, and leave yourself room to react. Fatigue impairs judgment nearly as much as alcohol — if you're tired, stop and rest before continuing.

B. Weather Driving

Slow down and lengthen your following distance in the rain. Stopping distances can double on a wet road, and standing water can cause a loss of traction with little warning.

C. Common Violations (indicative fines)

₱1,500–10,000No helmet (1st to 4th offense)
₱5,000Distracted driving (phone use)
₱1,000Disregarding a traffic signal
₱1,000Driving without OR/CR on hand

Fine amounts change periodically — always confirm the current schedule on the LTO website before relying on a figure.

D. Demerit Points

Violations add demerit points to your license. Build up 1–2 points and you're barred from adding a new license code for three months; 5 or more, a full year.

E. If You're in an Accident

  • Stop and check for injuries before moving anything.
  • Call for help — and document the scene with photos if it's safe to do so.
  • Exchange information with the other party and note down any witnesses.
Good to know. You can check and settle unsettled traffic violations directly through the LTMS Online Portal, without a trip to an LTO office.
